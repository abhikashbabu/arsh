7 warning signs of a heart problem you should never ignore

Dr. Priya Sharma 28 Oct 2026 6 min read

Heart problems do not always begin with a dramatic collapse. Many people have small warning signs for days or weeks before a serious event. Knowing these signs, and acting early, can protect your heart and even save your life.

1. Chest pain, pressure or tightness

A feeling of squeezing, heaviness or fullness in the centre of the chest is the most common sign. It may last more than a few minutes, or come and go. Do not dismiss it as gas or acidity if it comes with effort or stress.

2. Shortness of breath

Feeling breathless while resting, or after very light activity like climbing a few stairs, can mean the heart is not pumping well. It may come with or without chest discomfort.

3. Pain that spreads

Heart pain can travel to the left arm, shoulder, neck, jaw or back. Pain in these areas without any injury deserves a medical check.

Do not wait to see if it goes away on its own. With heart symptoms, time matters.

4. Cold sweat, nausea or light-headedness

Sudden sweating, feeling sick or dizzy, especially along with chest discomfort, can signal a heart attack.

5. Unusual tiredness

Feeling very tired for no clear reason, or being unable to do routine work you managed easily before, can be an early sign. Women, older adults and people with diabetes often notice fatigue, nausea or back pain instead of typical chest pain.

6. Palpitations

A racing, fluttering or irregular heartbeat that does not settle quickly should be checked with an ECG.

7. Swelling in the feet or ankles

Swelling that builds up through the day, especially with breathlessness, can be a sign that the heart is struggling to move blood effectively.

When to get emergency help

If chest pain is sudden, severe or lasts more than a few minutes, or comes with sweating, breathlessness or fainting, do not drive yourself. Call emergency services or our 24x7 emergency line at +91 73218 31111.

How to lower your risk

  • Check blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol at least once a year.
  • Stay away from tobacco in every form.
  • Be active for about 30 minutes on most days of the week.
  • Eat less salt and fried food, and more vegetables, fruit and fibre.
  • Sleep well and find healthy ways to manage stress.

If you have a family history of heart disease, diabetes or high blood pressure, start regular check-ups earlier. A short consultation with a cardiologist can tell you where you stand.
