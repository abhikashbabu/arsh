Angiography

Advanced cardiac catheterisation to find blocked or narrowed heart arteries, performed in our Cath Lab.

Overview

What is Coronary Angiography?

Angiography is a procedure that uses X-ray imaging to examine the blood vessels of the heart, checking for any restriction in blood flow. A contrast dye is injected and an X-ray machine captures a series of images to give a clear view of the arteries. It is performed at ARSH under the supervision of our cardiology team, with angioplasty available in the same sitting if a blockage is found.

When it's recommended

Angiography is generally performed when a patient has:

Symptoms of coronary artery disease, such as chest pain (angina)
Pain in the chest, jaw, neck or arm that cannot be explained by other tests
New or worsening chest pain (unstable angina)
Congenital heart disease
Unusual results on other cardiac tests
Other vascular problems or chest injuries
A heart valve problem that needs surgery
Patient questions

Angiography FAQs

For personal advice, please consult a qualified cardiologist.

Most patients feel only mild discomfort at the catheter access point. Local anaesthesia is used, and the procedure itself is not usually painful.

A diagnostic angiogram typically takes 30–60 minutes, though timing can vary depending on findings and whether further treatment is needed.

Many patients go home the same day or after an overnight observation, depending on the findings and whether a follow-up procedure such as angioplasty is performed.
